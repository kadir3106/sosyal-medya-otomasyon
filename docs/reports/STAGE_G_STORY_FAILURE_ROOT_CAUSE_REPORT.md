# Stage G story-failure root-cause review

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: the five **post-infrastructure** Stage G jobs only. This is a read-only diagnosis of existing artifacts and source code. No job, test, publication, or code change was made for this review. The original infrastructure-blocked attempts were not altered.

## Decision

**MIXED_ROOT_CAUSE.** The observable failures combine (1) missing or unsuitable evidence from a narrow Wikipedia-introduction retrieval path, (2) final claim wording that is broader or more causal than its attached evidence establishes, and (3) topic-entity drift in the replacement/research path. For two jobs the final HIGH-risk claim was discarded from the report on exception, so its underlying factual cause **cannot be classified more precisely**. This does **not** prove the fact gate is too strict or that all five topics are bad.

The shared, smallest repair to consider is **topic-entity-scoped evidence retrieval and replacement**: construct source queries from the actual topic and claim entities, reject irrelevant generic/entity pages and unrelated replacement facts, and keep the current fail-closed fact gate. Preserve the claim/evidence/decision trace before an early HIGH-risk exception so that this repair can be evaluated; this is diagnostic preservation, not a gate relaxation. Do not infer that this proposal alone would make all five jobs pass.

## Evidence limits and classification method

- The first three topic-review JSONs retain final script, final factual claims, risk, status, source **titles/URLs**, beat roles, and aggregate gates. They do **not** retain raw source extracts, search hits rejected by the verifier, confidence/notes, original draft claims, `ScriptQualityResult.claim_decisions`, `replacement_research`, `replacement_intents`, or individual fact/story-quality reasons. Thus a source title is not proof its text supports a claim. The two later JSONs retain only an exception containing a claim ID; no final script, claim wording, evidence, or preflight assessment survives.
- `prepare_review_story()` re-extracts and verifies the **final** script, then raises on an unsupported HIGH claim **before returning any claims** ([review_story.py](video-worker/app/documentary_v2/review_story.py)). `run_topic_review()` saves claims only after that return, and its exception handler saves only the exception text ([review_runner.py](video-worker/app/documentary_v2/review_runner.py)). The trace is therefore incomplete by construction, not merely missing from this review.
- `FactQualityGate` requires **every** final factual claim to be supported with evidence. Stage G calls any non-PASS result `UNSUPPORTED_CENTRAL_CLAIM`; there is no claim-level `central` flag or central-only check in this path. `source_assessment.verified_claims` is the **number of claim records**, not the number supported. The first three records count 4, 3, and 4; supported counts are 1, 0, and 0 respectively. A beat's `narrative_role` is only a narrative clue. Below, `CENTRAL: YES` means an analyst's inference that the beat is indispensable; `NO` means subordinate to that inferred spine. **Neither is a runtime designation.** For the two unlogged claims, even this inference is impossible.
- Categories below are assigned to **blocking claims**, including the MEDIUM claims that actually made the first three fact gates non-PASS. Each receives exactly one primary category. `I. OTHER` is used where the evidence gap prevents a responsible A–H attribution. Secondary categories are hypotheses or contributing observations, not alternate primaries. A claim can be plausibly verifiable in principle yet remain unsupported *in this run*.
- An external, read-only comparator confirms the glass topic is researchable: the [Glass Packaging Institute](https://www.gpi.org/what-glass) describes cullet crushing and contaminant removal; [Glass Alliance Europe](https://glassallianceeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/gmp-guidelines-for-glass-september-2015_1_file.pdf) describes sorted cullet mixed into the batch and melted. **These were not retrieved by the jobs, are not job evidence, and do not authorize a replacement example under the user's “already retrieved” rule.** They only substantiate the category-B distinction between a bad topic and failed retrieval.

## Cross-job findings

- **Primary-category claim counts (12 blocking claims):** A = 2 (LEGO/Disney), B = 3 (glass), C = 3 (LEGO/Disney/glass), I = 4 (LEGO/Disney/Eiffel/buyback); D/E/F/G/H = 0 **proven**. **Jobs affected:** A = 2, B = 1, C = 3, I = 4. Categories with zero proof remain possible, especially for unlogged claims; these are not exonerations.
- **Systemic versus topic-specific:** All five stopped in the verified-story phase, but the observable mechanisms differ. The repeated irrelevant `It`/`Today`/`Universal Studios Singapore` references, narrow Wikipedia-only introduction retrieval, broad final wording, and absent claim trace are systemic. Glass shows a clear retrieval-breadth failure; LEGO shows a verified but unrelated Hans Wilsdorf fact surviving consistency. Eiffel/buyback cannot be diagnosed substantively from stored artifacts.
- **Research breadth:** Insufficient for the glass process and unproven for the other topics. `FactVerificationProvider` searches English Wikipedia page titles and short introductory extracts (up to 2,500 characters); it does not use the external industry references above. The fallback query often uses the full question when a claim has no usable entities, then requires strong title-token overlap. This can return no page even for an ordinarily researchable process.
- **Central selection and risk:** No central-claim designation exists in the final verification path, so “central selection too aggressive” is **not measurable**; the Stage G failure name overstates what was checked. All observed claims in the first three jobs are MEDIUM, including two conspicuous absolutes. No evidence supports category E (risk too strict). The last two are HIGH by exception but their wording is unknown.
- **Claim strengthening:** The final LEGO exclusivity and Disney superlative/“entirely” sentences are broader than the support recorded. Whether strengthening happened at generation, local softening, or LLM refinement is **unknown** because draft and decisions are not persisted. Category D cannot be proved from the saved output alone.
- **Same-domain replacement:** Code has a research/replacement path, but per-job execution is not logged. The `story_domains.py` candidate list hardcodes Hans Wilsdorf/Rolex for ownership and related domains; its domain matching does not require LEGO entity identity. A supported Hans Wilsdorf sentence in the LEGO story, with `StoryConsistencyGate=PASS`, shows same-**semantic-domain** checking did not ensure same-**subject** relevance. The exact insertion route remains unproved. No already retrieved, supported same-subject replacement is recorded for any failed story.
- **Source bias:** The actual verifier draws from Wikipedia title matches/intros. Single-word sentence starters (`It`, `Today`) are extracted as proper entities; consequently unrelated title matches enter the evidence list. This is a concrete source-selection defect. It does not justify silently accepting an unsupported claim.
- **Preflight:** The first three were rejected at Stage G source preflight, but only *after* script generation/refinement/verification. The two HIGH exceptions occurred before that preflight. No saved evidence proves any of the five whole topics should have been rejected up front (category H).
